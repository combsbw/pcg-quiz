# Question authoring spec — read this fully before writing anything

You are authoring multiple-choice questions for flight and ground crews
studying the **Life Flight Network Patient Care Guidelines, edition 2026.09.15**.

The previous question set was generated by pattern-matching extracted text. It
failed, and the ways it failed are the reason you are doing this by reading:

* It asked backwards questions — *"An adult dose of 2 grams of TXA corresponds
  to which indication?"* Nobody thinks that way at 3am.
* It asked list-membership questions — *"Which of these is listed under BLS?"* —
  which test whether you have memorised a page, not whether you can treat a
  patient.
* It treated every section with the same template, so a ventilator page and an
  anaphylaxis page produced identically shaped questions.

**Your job is the opposite: read the actual pages, understand what matters
clinically in THIS section, and write questions a crew member would actually
need to answer.**

---

## 1. What to read

Your batch file lists your sections and their page numbers. For each section:

1. **Read the PDF pages visually** with the Read tool and the `pages` parameter
   (max 20 pages per call):
   `Read(file_path="<PDF path from your prompt>", pages="92-94")`
   This matters. Dose tables align an indication with its dose *by row*, and
   flowcharts carry treatment logic that exists nowhere in the text. A page's
   extracted text alone will mislead you.
2. **Cross-check exact strings** against `docs/data/content.json`, which holds
   the parsed outline (`guidelines[].structure`) and the formulary dose tables
   (`drugs[].doses`, `drugs[].info`). Use it to copy numbers exactly — never
   retype a dose from memory of the image.

If the two disagree, **the PDF page is the truth.**

---

## 2. Priorities

Weight your questions roughly like this. These came directly from the user:

| Priority | Focus | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Indications** — when is this done / given | ~20% |
| 2 | **Treatments** — what is actually done, in order, at which level | ~25% |
| 3 | **Drug dosages** — the number, the route, the ceiling | ~25% |
| 4 | **Procedural understanding** — steps, landmarks, confirmation | ~15% |
| 5 | Contraindications, drug class, PEARLS, assessment items, unique situations | ~15% |

Adapt to the section. A formulary page is mostly dosing. A procedure page is
mostly steps, landmarks and complications. A regulatory page is mostly criteria
and thresholds. **A section gets the questions it deserves, not a fixed menu.**

---

## 3. Framing rules

**Write the stem so it can be answered before the options are read.** If the
stem needs the options to make sense, it is wrong.

Name the full context: drug **and** indication **and** population where they
vary.

### Good
- `What is the adult dose of epinephrine for anaphylaxis with moderate or severe symptoms?`
- `In 5.02 Anaphylaxis, what is the pediatric IM epinephrine dose?`
- `A patient in cardiac arrest has refractory VF after three defibrillations. What does 3.03 direct next?`
- `What is the maximum single dose of diphenhydramine?`
- `At which landmark is a needle thoracostomy placed?`
- `Which finding requires a BLS crew to request ALS intercept under 2.02?`

### Forbidden
- Reverse lookups: *"A dose of 2 grams corresponds to which indication?"*
- List membership: *"Which of these is listed under ALS?"*
- *"Which of the following is true about X?"*
- "All of the above" / "None of the above" / "Both A and B"
- Negative stems unless the guideline itself is a prohibition
  (*"Which is a contraindication to…"* is fine; *"Which is NOT a step…"* is not)
- Anything whose answer is a heading, a page number, or a section title
- Two questions that are the same question reworded

---

## 4. Options

- Exactly **4 options**. One unambiguously correct.
- All four must be **the same kind of thing**: four doses, four drugs, four
  anatomic sites, four thresholds. Never mix a dose with a drug name.
- Distractors must be **real values from this document** — another dose of the
  same drug, the pediatric dose when you are asking for adult, a neighbouring
  threshold, another agent in the same class. Plausible to someone who half
  knows it.
- **Never invent a number.** If you need a fourth distractor and no real one
  exists, write a different question instead.
- Keep lengths similar. The correct answer must not be the longest or the most
  qualified option — that is a giveaway.
- No option may be a superset of another ("1 mg" vs "1 mg IV, may repeat").

---

## 5. Explanations

One or two sentences saying **why**, not just restating the answer. Include the
surrounding clinical condition where it matters.

> Good: "Anaphylaxis with moderate or severe symptoms gets 0.3 mg IM of the
> 1 mg/mL concentration, repeatable q15min. The IV/push-dose route is reserved
> for refractory anaphylaxis with hypotension."

> Bad: "The dose is 0.3 mg IM."

---

## 6. Output format

Write **one JSON file** to `authoring/out/<BATCH_ID>.json`:

```json
{
  "batch": "G05",
  "questions": [
    {
      "number": "5.02",
      "title": "Anaphylaxis & Allergic Reactions",
      "page": 92,
      "type": "dose",
      "care_level": "BLS",
      "population": "adult",
      "stem": "What is the adult IM epinephrine dose for anaphylaxis with moderate or severe symptoms?",
      "options": ["0.3 mg IM", "0.01 mg/kg IM", "1 mg IV", "0.1 mg SIVP over 2 min"],
      "answer_index": 0,
      "explanation": "…",
      "priority": "core",
      "tags": ["drug:epinephrine", "topic:anaphylaxis", "route:im"]
    }
  ]
}
```

Field rules:
- `number` / `title` — exactly as in your batch file.
- `page` — the page the answer is actually on, inside that section's range.
- `type` — one of: `indication`, `treatment`, `dose`, `max_dose`, `route`,
  `sequence`, `threshold`, `contraindication`, `scope`, `assessment`,
  `procedure`, `landmark`, `complication`, `equipment`, `class`, `adverse`,
  `mechanism`, `pearl`, `special`.
- `care_level` — `BLS`, `ALS`, `CCT`, `PEARLS`, or `null` for formulary/general.
- `population` — `adult`, `pediatric`, `neonatal`, `both`, or `null`.
- `priority` — `core` for what a crew must know cold; `detail` for the rest.
  Aim for roughly 60% core.
- `tags` — lowercase `prefix:value`. Use `drug:`, `topic:`, `route:`,
  `procedure:`, `population:` as they apply.

---

## 7. How many

Per section, by content density — **quality over volume**:

- A dense guideline (anaphylaxis, RSI, mechanical ventilation): **12–20**
- A normal guideline: **8–14**
- A short guideline (one band, half a page): **4–8**
- A formulary drug with several indications: **6–12**
- A single-indication drug: **4–7**

Do not pad. A section with little content gets few questions. If you find
yourself writing a weak question to hit a number, stop.

---

## 8. Before you finish — self-check every question

1. Could a knowledgeable crew member answer the stem with the options covered?
2. Is the answer **verbatim true** on the cited page? Re-read the page.
3. Is every distractor a real value from the document?
4. Are all four options the same kind of thing, similar in length?
5. Would this question change what someone does for a patient? If not, cut it.

Then run the validator and fix anything it reports:

```bash
python3 tools/validate_authored.py authoring/out/<BATCH_ID>.json
```

**Accuracy is the whole point.** This is dosing content for people who fly to
emergencies. A plausible-looking wrong number is worse than no question at all.
If you are unsure of a value, leave that question out and say so in your report.
